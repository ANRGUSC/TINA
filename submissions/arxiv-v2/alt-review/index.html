MajorK1 · confirmed

The composition law is not new

main-alt.tex:46-48, 135-140, 322-340, 373-385, 1033-1035

Marschak and Radner, Economic Theory of Teams (1972), Ch. 7 “The Team in a Dynamic Environment”, §4 treats a proper team in which each member observes its own function of one delayed state. The payoff has a fixed positive definite Q, the state follows a scalar autoregression, and normality is explicitly not assumed. Their eq. (4.22) is:

L(d) = (1 − w2d) V̂(0) + w2d L(0)

That is the boxed theorem at line 329 under this mapping:

Also in Chapter 7.

  • The rescaling proof, eqs. (4.8)–(4.14), is the step at lines 373-382.
  • The normalized law, eq. (4.21): “The value is the product of two terms: one depending on the delay alone and one on the information function alone.”
  • The stale-global corollary, eqs. (4.5)–(4.6), and a matrix-autoregression version, eqs. (3.46)–(3.47), which covers the unequal-rate formula.
  • §6, “Substitution of timeliness for completeness”: a one-parameter family where better information costs more delay, the first-order condition β′/β = log(1/w2) D′ (eq. 6.5), at most one interior solution under concavity and convexity, and an optimum that increases with w.
  • The symmetric-coupling example, eq. (6.6). At group size one it equals the manuscript’s 1 − ηS(0). A reviewer confirmed this in exact arithmetic against a brute-force team optimum (n = 6, q = 1, γ = 0.7 gives 104/153 from all three).

What remains the manuscript’s own. The weaker hypothesis (conditionally mean-zero innovations instead of independent ones), continuous time, a general affine map K, the graph-radius family, the explicit crossover delay, the closed-form optimum for exponential omission, the three-length canonical radius formula, and the ring and four-graph examples. The literature reviewer did not find the radius formula elsewhere, but that search was not exhaustive.

Field context. Marschak–Radner is the standard reference for static teams. Rediscovery of a result in a cited standard reference is a common ground for rejection at IEEE TAC, TIT or Automatica. Comparable papers state such identities as known lemmas with attribution.

  2. Cite eq. (6.5) for marginal balance and eq. (6.6) for the aggregate-coupling share.
  3. Change “we obtain an exact rule” in the abstract and “we prove” in contribution 1.
  4. Replace the “distinctive object” sentence at line 1035 with what is new.
  5. Reorder the contributions so the radius results lead.

Issue

The continuum model invokes a finite-graph theorem

main-alt.tex:741-785, 813-850

The composition theorem is stated and proved for a finite-dimensional stacked state with matrix K and Q. The canonical radius theorem is proved by substitution into the exponential-omission proposition, which begins “Under Theorem [composition]”. The canonical model has a field-valued state, an integral target and a continuous radius. The paper never says the law holds there, and never verifies the temporal conditional-mean assumption for the field.

Why it is only medium. The results are true. If the field is jointly Gaussian in space and time, the gap closes in three lines: the innovation θ(·,t) − aθ(·,t−τ) is uncorrelated with the whole old field, hence independent of it, and the uncoupled loss makes each location a single-agent projection. A discretised two-time-slice field matches the formula to about 1e-5.

Why it still matters. “Gaussian spatial field” at line 744 does not quite say jointly Gaussian in space and time. Under the weaker reading the law can fail: the verifier built a field that is Gaussian in space at each time with the right lag covariance, for which the law predicts a loss ratio of 0.833 and the true optimum is 0.736. The predecessor (v2-h) said “The zero-mean Gaussian field has covariance” at main.tex:1107.

Field context. Papers that prove a general result in finite dimensions and then give a continuum closed form normally add a short remark that the proof carries over and verify each hypothesis. A referee would ask for that paragraph; it is a minor-revision request.

Suggested fix. Add one remark before the self-similarity lemma that states the field is a zero-mean jointly Gaussian space-time process, notes the innovation is independent of the whole old field, and says the composition proof applies per location with K replaced by the kernel integral, for continuous r ≥ 0. Put “Gaussian” in the lemma and theorem statements.

Evidence

Derivation (my own). For a zero-mean jointly Gaussian field with the separable covariance, let eps(z) = theta(z,t) - a theta(z,t-tau). Then Cov(eps(z), theta(z',t-tau)) = sigma^2 exp(-|z-z'|/l_s)(a - a) = 0 for all z, z', so eps is independent of the whole old field. Hence x*(z,t) = a x*(z,t-tau) + int w eps, the cross term vanishes, and E[(int w eps)^2] = (1-a^2) Var(x*) by stationarity. Pointwise loss is (q/2)[(1-a^2) Var(x*) + a^2 Var(x* | O_r)], which is the composition law.

Justification

The results are true and the gap closes in a few lines, so this is not a major finding.

Closing argument: if the field is zero-mean and jointly Gaussian in (z, t) with the separable covariance, then eps(z) = theta(z,t) - a theta(z,t-tau) satisfies Cov(eps(z), theta(z', t-tau)) = sigma^2 exp(-|z-z'|/l_s)(a - a) = 0 for all z, z'. By joint Gaussianity eps is independent of the sigma-field of the whole old field, and hence of every interval observation O_r(z, t-tau). Because the loss is uncoupled, each location is a single-agent L^2 projection problem. Then x*(z,t) = a x*(z,t-tau) + int w eps, the cross term vanishes, and E[(int w eps)^2] = (1 - a^2)Var(x*) by stationarity. This gives the pointwise loss (q/2)[(1 - a^2)Var(x*) + a^2 Var(x*(z) | O_r(z))], which is the composition law.

Why it still matters: without joint space-time Gaussianity, or an explicit conditional-mean assumption on the field, the stated hypothesis is insufficient by the paper's own criterion at lines 297-301. Issue

The final takeaway reads: 'the theory yields a practical design heuristic: expand information scope as long as the marginal decision value of broader information exceeds the predictive value lost in obtaining it.' This goes beyond the results in two ways. Field context

A TAC or Automatica referee would probably accept the model as a stylized baseline given the disclosures elsewhere, but would ask that the final design statement be conditioned on it. Calling it a 'heuristic' softens the unimodality point but not the architecture point.

Optional strengthenings

Below the reporting bar; offered for the authors’ discretion.

  • The law in reduction form, R∞ − R(Ar,τ) = a2[R∞ − R(Ar,0)] with epoch-specific quantities, holds without stationarity.
  • The composition proof needs only a scalar a(τ); the exponential form enters only in the crossover and radius formulas.
  • In the coupling example, equal variances are not needed. Independence is needed: with uncorrelated but dependent components the stated local rule is not optimal.
  • The coherence-time monotonicity seen in the graph sweeps is guaranteed for any omission curve, because log G has increasing differences in (r, T).
  • For the Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck case, a short calculation (not numerically checked) suggests older readings of the same neighborhood are redundant given the latest snapshot.
